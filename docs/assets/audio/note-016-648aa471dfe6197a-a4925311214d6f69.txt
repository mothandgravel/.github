We have no account of useful computing work, no controlled intervention, and no grounds to call 1 hour a better service. The pair shows why 1 ratio cannot carry the whole account. Keep the units attached. The file contains power in kilowatts. Energy needs time. We treat each reported power as a point sample. The publisher does not specify the instrument's averaging window. Between neighboring readings, we draw a straight line, clip it at the hour boundary, and integrate the trapezoid underneath it. The result is an estimate in kilowatt hours. Every component uses the same valid intervals. Missing or negative components leave time uncovered. A gap over 90 seconds also stays uncovered. We do not fill silence with zero demand. The selected week has complete coverage under that rule. The larger archive has missing values and outlying ratios worth inspecting. The hourly ratio divides the two energy totals. Averaging the sample ratios would answer another question. It produces a nearby number here, but proximity is a poor reason to lose the definition. The publisher's time stamps carry no time zone. We preserve their clock, including microseconds, without quietly turning it into finished time. Weather is matched only to an earlier reading on that same source clock within 90 seconds. More convincing materials should make that distinction easier to read, not easier to overlook. A reserve is a choice. A separate branch lets the recorded hour meet a chosen grid cap and battery. At the second hour demand, a 2.5 MW cap leaves work for the reserve. A 500 kWh battery starts at 80%, keeps 10% in reserve, and delivers 92% of the energy it discharges. With the branch's 1 MW output limit, it supplies 322 kWh. Conversion loss is 28 kWh. The remaining accounting shortfall is about 130.992 kWh. Those assumptions are ours. They model neither a generator starting nor a server failing. There is no cooling response, charging, or workload rescheduling hidden behind the button. Change a choice, and the branch must be calculated again. Export it, and the assumptions travel with it.
