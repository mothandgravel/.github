# Weather passage / 11 November 2026

Saved civilian environmental context for Coastal watch. This packet contains 144 EUMETSAT cloud-product slots across the Finnish civil day, twelve Estonian precipitation composites around 10 UTC, and pointers to unchanged FMI buoy observations in the original source notebook. It is not a live feed or an incident reconstruction.

The civil-day window is 10 November 22:00 UTC inclusive to 11 November 22:00 UTC exclusive. Every original download has a SHA-256 HTTP receipt with separate request and completion times. Neither retrieval clock establishes first publication or original station reception.

## Clouds

Original WMS PNG bytes from the EUMETView `mtg_fd:rgb_geocolour` layer are unchanged. The 1,100 × 400 `CRS:84` crop covers 23–28.5° E, 59–61° N. The complete time-domain response enumerates the selected ten-minute product slots. A product timestamp is not the acquisition time of every pixel. The website adds contextual coastlines, labels and layout cropping; it does not infer cloud velocity or rainfall from RGB colours.

## Estonian radar

Original HDF5 composites from Keskkonnaagentuur's public archive are retained under [CC BY 4.0](https://keskkonnaportaal.ee/et/avaandmed/meteoroloogiliste-radarite-andmestik). The source nodes are `eesyr,eehar`: Sürgavere and Harku, combined into one derived weather product. These are not vessel-radar recordings.

The inspected quantity is `RATE`, a radar-derived rain-rate estimate in mm/h. It is not surface rain-gauge accumulation. Calibration comes from the files: gain 1, offset 0, no-data 65535, below-detection 0. The latter two codes both have unknown physical values in the inspection model; below detection is not reported as a measured zero. Dataset product-interval labels and the file's product clock are retained separately. The brief supplied intervals may describe composite generation; underlying radar-sweep and per-pixel acquisition remain unknown.

Each 720 × 720 float32 matrix is copied unchanged in native row/column order to deterministic gzip, little endian. The browser verifies the decompressed length and SHA-256 before sampling. PNGs use seven discrete positive-value colour bands: up to 0.05, 0.2, 0.5, 1, 2 and 5 mm/h, then an open-ended class greater than 5 mm/h. Missing cells are transparent over a hatch and below detection is blue-grey. The displayed image is resized with nearest-neighbour sampling; every original native cell remains available in the inspector.

**Map registration is explicit; the conflicting metadata is not repaired.** The supplied ellipsoidal Mercator CRS and LL/UR outer-pixel corners anchor a rectangular projected grid. Its projected increments are inferred from those bounds. The supplied metre scales and conflicting LR latitude remain in the notebook but are not used to position the grid. Native row order is north to south and column order west to east, as defined by [ODIM 2.4.2, Table 5 and Section 5.2](https://www.eumetnet.eu/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/ODIM_H5_v2.4.2_final.pdf). The ellipsoid is the one in the source (`a=6378137`, `rf=298.257224`), not spherical Web Mercator. See [PROJ's Mercator definition](https://proj.org/en/stable/operations/projections/merc.html).

This is a **studio inference**, not a provider-confirmed correction or a survey. The [agency's published code example](https://keskkonnaportaal.ee/et/avaandmed/radariandmestik/radariandmete-kirjeldus) places the same LL/UR bounds directly in PlateCarree. We retain that as an inspectable alternative latitude for each selected native cell. Their separation shows sensitivity to the interpretation; it is not a confidence interval, accuracy radius, or exhaustive list of possible registrations. Four corners and declared scales still fail to reconcile. No undocumented `lat_ts`, scale factor or translation was fitted to make the coast look right.

Most of the roughly twofold difference between declared and projected metres is compatible with Mercator's ground/projected scale near 60° N; it is not evidence of a twofold geographic error. The declared/corner span ratios are retained, along with their spherical-equivalent true-scale latitudes. The two ratios differ by about 3.7%, so a single uniform scale cannot reconcile both; the LR latitude also remains unexplained. A ground-distance convention is plausible but has not been confirmed. Corner-normalised registration itself would not change under a uniform Mercator scale factor. The literal-metre comparator below is a diagnostic of that literal interpretation, not a claim of a 58 km real-world position error.

All twelve matrices carry a geometric consistency check. We select data cells adjacent to no-data in four-neighbour topology, excluding the outer two rows/columns that truncate the coverage. Each boundary-cell centre is compared by WGS84 geodesic distance to the nearer of the two agency-documented radar positions and their nominal 250 km ranges. For the first composite, the median absolute range residual is approximately 0.512 km with corner-anchored Mercator, 2.246 km with the agency PlateCarree example, and 57.816 km when the metre scales are used literally. This supports a choice among these interpretations; the source mask is not independent geolocation evidence, and nominal radar range, beam geometry and quantisation do not establish actual positional accuracy. No calibration is fitted. Every frame's sample count, median, 90th percentile and maximum residual are saved in `georeferencing.footprintChecks`.

The two corner-based candidates share longitudes, so the range test does not discriminate east-west placement. Their roughly 4–11 km latitude separation across the map can coexist with small range residuals. Northern/southern coverage extremes lie beyond the saved rectangle and are excluded. The best residual approaches the roughly 0.8 km ground-cell quantisation; it must not be read as sub-kilometre geolocation accuracy.

The geographic map PNGs use inverse mapping from each 1,100 × 400 CRS:84 output-pixel centre into this projected native grid, then select the containing native cell. Rain rate is never interpolated. Positive cells retain the seven native palette classes. Missing cells have a diagonal display hatch; below-detection cells have neutral horizontal ruling, so they differ by pattern rather than only opacity. Neither supplies a physical rain rate. Opacity blends display classes with the cloud image; numeric estimates come from the native-cell inspector. Original native PNGs and float32 files stay unchanged and separately inspectable. Approximate cell centres and the alternative placement are exported with unknown accuracy. A mapped cell does not measure surface rainfall at a buoy. Scripts `coastal_radar_placement.py` and `build_coastal_weather.py` reproduce this decision offline, including independent Pyproj coordinate controls checked by the browser model tests.

Wave arrows transform the reported geographic from-bearing into the local tangent of this equal-degree CRS:84 crop, using ellipsoidal meridional and prime-vertical radii. A raw compass angle would be wrong against this non-conformal coastline. Arrow length is schematic; no wave velocity is inferred. Crossed buoy marks mean missing height; absence of an arrow means missing direction. Selecting a buoy uses its exact FMI station coordinates, while the nearby radar cell retains approximate placement and its own product clock.

## Buoys

The original FMI source packet supplies hourly requested samples for Gulf of Finland, Suomenlinna and Orrengrund. Values and raw `NaN` strings are unchanged. Original XML files and their earlier receipts remain in `data/coastal-sources/2026-11-11/raw/`; the source packet's first capture does not supply a download-completion timestamp, and this is not backfilled.

FMI defines direction as **from**, clockwise degrees from north; modal period is the dominant wave period. The reported directional spread is not an uncertainty confidence interval. The sinusoidal drawing is explicitly a schematic of the reported statistics, not a measured waveform or recovered spectrum. [FMI definitions](https://en.ilmatieteenlaitos.fi/guidance-to-observations); [FMI open-data licence](https://en.ilmatieteenlaitos.fi/open-data-licence).

## Rebuild and inspect

Install the pinned decoder requirements from `tools/specimens/coastal-weather-requirements.txt` into an isolated environment. Run `python scripts/build_coastal_weather.py --check` to rebuild and compare the notebook, derived matrices/images and provenance inventory entirely offline. The explicit network acquisition tool is `scripts/capture_coastal_weather.py`; it caps responses at 2 MB, checks storage, keeps TLS verification enabled, and preserves prior captures.

The cursor never chooses a later product label, radar product-interval end label or buoy sample label. Cloud per-pixel acquisition remains unknown, so the no-later-label policy cannot establish pixel acquisition before the cursor. Cloud slots expire after ten minutes; radar requires a product-interval end label within five minutes and remains inside the saved strip; buoy hours select the corresponding original sample even when missing. Dedicated scan selection and playback visit all twelve radar records, including the last at its exact supplied end label. No interpolation fills gaps. Full notebook, original bytes and individual inspection exports retain these boundaries.
