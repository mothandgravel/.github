Where should a visitor check-in begin?
A meeting guest knows their host. A courier may only know the delivery. Can the first screen welcome both without asking either to guess?

What we left aside
A single search for everything.
In this fictional study, we set aside one search box for hosts, appointments and deliveries. It looked simpler, but expected visitors to understand the building’s categories before they could begin.
The route stays here with its reason. We could revisit it if a test shows people already understand those categories.

      

      
02 / YOUR NEXT DECISION
Which beginning should we test?
Host first gives meeting guests a direct route. Reason first gives different arrivals a visible entrance, but adds a choice for everyone.
We have no usability result yet. Choosing an approach here only prepares a local draft for a possible test.

03 / WHAT RESISTED
One arrival isn’t every arrival.
The working doubt: a host search may feel obvious to someone who visits offices often. That assumption may fail for deliveries, contractors or someone arriving without a named contact.
This is a hypothesis in an example study, not an observed client result.
