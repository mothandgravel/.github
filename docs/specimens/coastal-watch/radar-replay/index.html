AROUND THE REPORTED POSITION / RECORDED RADAR

Near is a place.
It is not an identity.

Open the returns around this AIS position at each station. A bright square may be worth a closer look. It does not tell us which vessel put it there.

The circle is an inspection radius, not a confidence region or hull outline. Each view stays in its station’s local coordinates. No automatic association is made.

The floor filters the positive-bin count and nearest-bin outline; the original raster stays visible. It uses each station’s fixed logarithmic display scale, not calibrated power or a detection threshold. Brightness cannot be compared as equal strength across stations.

A RADAR PATCH / A SEPARATE AIS ACCOUNT

Does the return
go with it?

Follow the texture around a selected radar bin. Keep the AIS account beside it. A return that stays put may be a fixed structure, clutter, or a vessel that is not moving.

This is a new specimen experiment. It matches radar texture without AIS guidance. A retained patch is a hypothesis, not a confirmed vessel track or a shore classification.

Choose “Follow this return over time” above, or select any positive measured bin in the raw inspector and follow it. Either station can supply a seed; no track is carried between stations. Changing the AIS ID or choosing a new inspected seed clears this account. Export first to keep its branches.

For a recorded example, open a close competition at Reiherstieg, frame 2. This patch is away from the selected AIS position; it demonstrates texture ambiguity only.

A fixed 5 × 5 seed patch, searched within a square around the last retained bin. Log-amplitude correlation must reach 0.70 and lead the next candidate by 0.10. At least 9 measured pairs and 70% of the seed’s measured bins are required. These are experimental settings, not calibrated probabilities. An uncertain step stops the chain; there is no automatic reacquisition.

PART OF COASTAL WATCH
The account
gets another layer.
The transport workbench follows delivery. The identity ledger keeps competing assertions. This desk opens recorded measurements and the assumptions used to compare them.
These radar recordings remain separate from the live AIS transport journal and the fictional identity rehearsal. No subject is silently carried between them.
What these returns can carry.
The published extracts use Raivari’s existing HAXR extractor, validator, AIS selector, timestamp-pairing and station-continuity code. The 128 × 128 raster uses 31.25 m bins over ±2 km. Amplitude is shown on a fixed logarithmic scale within each station’s extract. Brightness is not calibrated power and cannot be compared as equal strength across stations.
No station-axis calibration, spatial fusion, automatic radar detection, vessel association or spoofing judgement is performed. UTC time-of-day comes from the files; no calendar date is inferred.
