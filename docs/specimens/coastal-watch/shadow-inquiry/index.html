What exists in Raivari
The Shadow Fleet Sentinel architecture and implementation combine RF heuristics, optional radar correlation and an audit chain. The current process returns no result without RF metrics. The simulator generates those metrics and guards their use behind an option that defaults off. Native and JavaScript fallback analyzers differ; this review does not establish equivalent outputs. Public AIS captures do not include those metrics. Its fixed score weights do not establish a calibrated spoofing probability.
The station-continuity replay already distinguishes shared AIS lineage. The radar tracker is a nearest-neighbour hold-position baseline, without covariance or motion prediction. The real queue, burst sync and receipt journal remain available in the two-station workbench.
This public desk is an original, synthetic model informed by those contracts. It does not execute the native Sentinel analyzer or publish private implementation code. An audit signature would establish a different property from the truth of a sensor reading.


Read the model and its limits
Four stored deliveries represent three originals in two source families. The relay retains A-01’s lineage. Received time gates knowledge. All positions use an invented local east/north metre plane, with no real coastline, ship, registry or sanctions data.
For comparison only, AIS moves east at an assumed 8 m/s. Its base position bound is 35 m; velocity uncertainty is 1.2 m/s. Radius = 35 + 1.2 × (absolute time difference + clock uncertainty) + 8 × clock uncertainty. Radar has an assumed 55 m bound. Bounds are deterministic illustrations, not measured accuracy or confidence regions. The mean corrected radar epoch cannot exceed its receipt time; the correction control is bounded accordingly.
Separated bounds mean disagreement under those assumptions. Overlap does not prove association, independence, innocence or wrongdoing. No fused position or risk percentage is produced. Exports are unsigned and contain the full labelled fixture, including later records, separately from the selected historical view.
Next integration: retain original identifiers and timestamps from licensed AIS and radar recordings; attach receiver coverage, acquisition clocks and calibration; link dated identity assertions to verified IMO records; preserve source lineage when analysts add registry or sanctions evidence. Each source needs an acquisition and use agreement. No such integration or operational validation is claimed here.
