A QUESTION OF WHAT REACHES US

The connection
came back.
Did the picture?

The wire can be there while the view is missing. A small reassuring label has a surprisingly large job.

LiveConnect’s streaming code keeps connection state separate from the age of a measurement. Here, we turn that distinction over in our hands.

Inspect the decision ↗

A source-based design study. Invented readings run through a selected measurement helper. The two surfaces below are presentation sketches. This page opens no stream, camera or microphone.

WHAT CHANGED

A recent reading.

The sample is within the source’s freshness window. It records frames; it does not tell us whether the picture is useful.

Open the working record

Connection fixture: connected. Measurement helper: current.

We replay the selected source helper with authored inputs and a fixed clock. Its rule distinguishes current, stale and unavailable measurements. The connection is a separate fixture held at connected; no connection library runs here.

At exactly 15 seconds the complete fixture is still current. It becomes stale when older than 15 seconds. This threshold belongs to the selected source, rather than every streaming interface.

These are sketches and helper results, not captured telemetry or a shipped product change. A current measurement does not establish picture quality. We have not yet observed whether people understand the split more easily.

02 / THE DECISION WE ARE TRYING

Let the old number
keep its age.

We keep the connection label narrow. We give the measurement a name and an age. When it is old, the number stays available as history, with its uncertainty beside it.

The surface becomes a little busier. That is the trade we need to examine: does the added distinction help someone choose their next step, or leave them reading diagnostics?

A question for the next trial: ask someone what they know at eighteen seconds, and what they would do next.
