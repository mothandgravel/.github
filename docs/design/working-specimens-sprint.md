The owner approved this sprint after the first-engagement work and widened selection to the portfolio strategy and Navivra repositories. The goal is to show what the studio actually makes while keeping design questions, rather than a client-logo portfolio, at the centre.

## Critique and alternatives

1. **Editorial covers as product evidence.** Attractive but inappropriate for this section: a conceptual cover cannot show an interface or a recorded result. Rejected.
2. **A long, pinned full-screen project tour.** It adds a scrolling sequence and images before someone can inspect the question. It would make phone reading and the opening budget harder to preserve. Rejected.
3. **Three visible specimens with native working records.** The output, question and source scope remain together. Details expand with the keyboard, without script or spatial navigation. Chosen.

The desktop composition gives three different materials equal places on the bench. At laptop/phone widths the full artifacts stack; no panel is cropped to manufacture a thumbnail. Full-size image links keep the original render inspectable. Scope is visible in each figure caption, with the exact interpretation available in the native disclosure. The site source and brand marks remain available in a studio-drawer disclosure rather than occupying the project places.

A small optional module adds pointer handling to the artifact planes. It is fetched only after initial loading and when the bench enters the viewport. Generated CSS trims syntax whitespace while preserving strings and calc spacing; the homepage drops tag-line indentation to keep the response small. There is no viewer dependency, web font or remote data fetch. Images are lazy-loaded with dimensions; authored styles are included through the existing homepage builder. No user actions, AI calls, device activity or live recovery are run by the page. No photos or footage are generated as evidence. Existing private-enquiry and client-room work remains separate.

## ARRVL / motion notes

The follow-up [ARRVL motion study](arrvl-motion-study.md) records additional live observations and a focused light-and-handling sprint before the proper 3D moth.

The owner drew attention to ARRVL’s crisp liveliness and almost three-dimensional feel. Direct browser review of https://arrvl.studio/ on 2026-10-13 showed an orange grid introduction, a dark textured visual with isolated white marks, and a light selected-work composition with large type and separate image/label surfaces. Navigation and scrolling were inspected; these observations are not measurements of animation timing, user response or loading performance.

The translation is a handling light, with the existing bone, charcoal and brass character:

- Give the material a depth plane; hold captions, questions and controls still.
- Respond to inspection, then settle. Mouse movement tilts the plane by at most three degrees and lifts it three pixels. Leaving, keyboard focus, lost visibility or a changed motion preference resets it.
- Opening a working record lights the card edge and plate. No animated height or scroll choreography is needed.
- Keep a complete resting composition. Native records and full-size links work without JavaScript. Touch and reduced-motion settings retain the static presentation.

The module coalesces pointer events into one animation-frame update, observes visibility and has no continuous animation loop. The browser checks compare material movement with stationary text, then test reset, keyboard opening, reduced-motion changes and coarse-pointer behaviour. No claim is made that this captures ARRVL’s complete motion system; it is a focused first pass for Moth & Gravel.
