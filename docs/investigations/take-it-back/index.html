Did it happen,
and can I
take it back?

A press disappears. A reply never comes. A whole staircase goes down in one click. The work may already be done, and trying again may do it twice.

We keep asking what a person should see before their next move, and whether the way back matches the thing they believe they did. Seven things made here ask it too.

SKYSTACK BUILDER / ONE UNDO

One gesture. One undo.

Place a stamp on a small island. Every cell is checked before anything is spent. A placement becomes one entry in history, and Undo either takes the whole thing back or says plainly that it could not.

Then follow the seven pieces of work below.

This toy needs JavaScript. In words: a stamp is placed whole or not at all, and one undo takes back the whole stamp, or refuses and keeps its record.

A rules sketch written from the source study in Field Note 008. No game code, art or assets; not a capture of SkyStack Builder.

01 / AJO

Ajo
Prototype

No answer
is not a no.

Checked plans for agents, with their execution recorded. In one saved simulator case the reply is lost: the host cannot confirm the outcome. Querying the original result recovers the answer, and the receiver still records a single application.

A general “try again” would have left too much unsaid. The record keeps the difference between not knowing and not done.

02 / SKYSTACK BUILDER

SkyStack Builder
Prototype

One gesture,
one undo.

An island builder for Windows. Its stamp tools check a whole footprint before anything is spent, place several blocks as one entry in history, and undo that entry as one thing.

If a block is no longer where the stamp left it, Undo reports that it failed and keeps the entry rather than winding the world back by guesswork. The stamp at the top of this page follows those rules.

03 / DECISION DOCKET

Decision Docket
In production for our own work

An uncertain save,
sent once.

Our own tool for the decisions and evidence behind software work, in production for our own work. When a hand-off is saved and the answer never arrives, the retry replays the same request instead of making a second hand-off.

Undo has the same care: restoring an older hand-off keeps the Undo for a newer one.

Described from our own change history. The real workspace holds private work and is not shown.

04 / RAIVARI-RPC

raivari-rpc
Study

The agent asks.
The host decides.

An exploration of how an agent and its host talk. An agent may ask for an action; the host decides whether it happens.

05 / PAJA

paja
Internal tool

Failed, cancelled
or timed out.

Spare machines on one network, building together. Its results tell a failed step apart from a failure of the machines, a cancellation and a timeout, so whoever runs it again knows what actually happened.

Read the field note on reassuring signals ↗

06 / LUOTSI

Luotsi
Internal tool

The step
that failed.

Drives Android devices from a laptop over adb, one step at a time. Each command answers in structured JSON, and a run keeps its screenshots, logs and replay bundle, so a failure can be read after the device is gone.

A failed step leaves an envelope that names the step, the error and the artifacts it kept, instead of a red line and silence.

Read “Cancelled is a result.” ↗

07 / VERSTAS

Verstas
Prototype

Cancelled,
before finalization.

Looks at the workspaces on a disk and plans their cleanup, without deleting anything. Its plan has three answers: ready, review required or blocked.

A cancelled scan says that it was cancelled before finalization. The last finished inventory stays in place instead of being replaced by half of a new one. Verstas is a working name.
