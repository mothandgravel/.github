What we
take on.

Three lines of work, and the kind of question each one starts from. These are questions a client might bring, not records of work done.

Interface design

We take the moment a person hesitates and make the beginning, the picture, or the control clear enough to act on.

A CLIENT MIGHT ASK

  • I can see the problem and still cannot start. What should the first screen ask?
  • The status says everything is fine. What can a person actually know from it?
  • Something on the screen moves while the work is unfinished. What has to stay put?

Cloud and mobile software

We design and build software a person can understand, question, and keep using, in the cloud or on a phone.

A CLIENT MIGHT ASK

  • This started as a small script. What has to stay true when people begin to rely on it?
  • The work is scattered across tools. What is the smallest version worth putting in someone’s hands?
  • Two parts of the product almost agree. Which difference should the person see?

Connected devices

When a screen has to speak for a machine, we decide what the person sees, what can wait, and what the software must not invent.

A CLIENT MIGHT ASK

  • A reading arrives, then the stream stops. What should the last picture still say?
  • The device and the app disagree about what just happened. What should a person see before trying again?
  • The machine knows more than the screen can show. What stays in view?

WHO IT’S FOR
Someone with one uncertainty about an interface, a piece of software, or a device, who wants material they can inspect before deciding whether to go on. The studio is in Finland and takes questions from anywhere.

WHAT YOU RECEIVE

  1. 01 / THE QUESTION

    A boundary.

    The one question, narrowed, and a note on what would make this piece useful.

  2. 02 / THE PIECE

    One thing to inspect.

    An annotated review, a sketch, a working prototype, or a technical experiment. The form is chosen and fixed before the work starts. One engagement includes one of these, not all four.

  3. 03 / THE NEXT MOVE

    A next move.

    What the material shows, what is still open, and a recommendation: build, revise, look more closely, or stop.

First, a conversation.

Before any paid work, both sides agree the question, the piece, the fee, the timing, and where this work ends. Further work is a separate choice.

04 — THE PERSON AT THE BENCHSLIDEEP / FINLAND

Many projects.
One curiosity.

Behind Moth & Gravel is slideep: a maker of strange, useful things. More than 25 years spent getting complicated systems to work together—and still drawn to the odd question.

The work moves between screens and the things they touch: cloud services, mobile software, relays, microcontrollers and physical terminals. Some questions arrive as code. Others arrive with wires.

AI is part of the making; architecture, constraints and judgment stay close to hand. Field Notes keep the useful discoveries and stubborn questions in view. The questions travel between projects. So do we.

What does the studio make?

Independent software and useful tools. The work crosses cloud services, mobile interfaces, microcontrollers and physical devices. Field Notes show the design questions inside that work.

Where is Moth & Gravel based?

Based in Finland. Open to questions from anywhere. The studio’s public record lives here: source, materials and studies from across the workbench.

How can I get in touch?

Email hello@mothandgravel.com. Tell us what you are trying to make and where it gets difficult. Prefer to think first? Shape a brief, then send it by email or keep it. A public GitHub issue also works; it requires an account, so keep private details out of it.
