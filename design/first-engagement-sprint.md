# First engagement / small enough to finish

## The question

Can someone new to Moth & Gravel tell what they could bring, what the first piece of work would leave them with, and how that work begins? The landing already has studio character, design studies, an editable question journey and a fictional return visit. This sprint supplies the missing boundary between interest and an engagement.

## Alternatives and critique

1. **A menu of services.** Reviews, prototypes and technical experiments become packages a person must choose before knowing which fits. This would ask the customer to solve the scoping question. Rejected.
2. **Another introductory page.** A complete explanation sits behind a new link and another navigation hop, while the landing still leaves the engagement obscure. Rejected as the primary route.
3. **One work sheet on the landing.** A question, inspectable material and a reasoned next move describe the engagement as one bounded piece. A native disclosure shows how it applies to an explicitly fictional example. Chosen.

## The resulting design

The section sits after the current workbench material and before the studio principles. It uses the existing heading, bone/charcoal/brass tokens and native HTML. Three parts belong to one bordered sheet rather than separate package cards. On narrow screens they stack in reading order. The example can be opened with the keyboard and remains usable without JavaScript.

The visible pre-work boundary names the question, material, fee, timing and stopping point. Further work requires another choice. No fixed package, guaranteed finding, price, appointment, availability or response time is invented. The owner has approved this first-engagement direction; the public wording is proposed through this PR.

The contact section links back to the explanation. Its existing enquiry and brief routes remain functional. The final brief step includes a native disclosure explaining how a conversation becomes agreed work, without leaving the page, copying answers, classifying the question into a service or altering the editable brief. Timing, budget and existing material remain optional form follow-ups.

The concrete example uses the mending notebook: uncertainty about naming damage, two sketches and a revised way to show the worn part, and a proposed observation for deciding what to test. It claims no client delivery or player/user result. Specimen scenarios stay in personal making, creative tools and playful systems; workplace reception and access flows are outside this studio's example material. Employment background is not part of public copy.

Private enquiry delivery and a real authenticated client room remain separate work. This sprint adds no backend, booking, payment or automatic response. It does not imply a browser brief was received by the studio.

## Review criteria

- The material and next move remain visible without opening the example.
- The example clearly distinguishes proposed observation from a result.
- Before paid work, someone can find the fee/timing/scope boundary.
- A keyboard user can inspect the disclosure and reach the real question journey.
- The editable brief remains literal; opening the explanation does not commit or send it.
- Both themes, phone layouts, script-free reading and the current opening budget hold.

## Human observation still to run

Give the landing to someone with an unclear idea and someone with an existing interface difficulty. Ask what they could bring, what they expect to receive and what happens before paid work. Ask whether continuing is required. Record their answers and points of hesitation without explaining the section first. This is a proposed comprehension study; automated checks do not establish commercial conversion or human understanding.
